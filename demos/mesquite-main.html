The Lofts

Industrial bones. Deliberate everything else.

The building spent seventy years moving produce through Tucson. The renovation kept what worked — brick, steel, daylight by the wall — and rebuilt the rest to a standard the original foreman would respect.

  • Exposed 1948 brick

    Original masonry, sealed and left honest — every loft wears a different pattern of it.

  • Steel sash windows

    Nine feet tall, re-glazed for the desert, aimed at the mountains or the marquee lights.

  • Quartz & steel kitchens

    Restaurant-grade ranges, quartz counters, open shelving on blackened steel.

  • Smart entry, standard

    Keyless doors, smart thermostats, and fiber internet ready on day one.

Amenities

Built for people with somewhere to be.

  • The roof

    A full-block deck with grills, shade sails, and the best free show in town: sunset over the Tucsons.

  • The gear room

    Secure bike storage, a workbench, and lockers for climbing rope and river gear.

  • Mezzanine cowork

    Desks and call booths above the lobby — fiber fast, coffee close.

  • Lobby counter

    A local roaster runs the front counter — residents drink at cost, regulars keep it loud.

  • Practice room

    Soundproofed, drum-kit ready, bookable by the hour. Downtown has gigs; you should take one.

  • Dog wash

    A proper wash bay by the freight door, towels included, judgment not.

  • Package hall

    Cold storage for groceries, oversize shelves for the bike you ordered anyway.

  • EV & garage

    Gated garage across the alley with Level-2 charging on every row.

Downtown

Your block does the commuting.

Mesquite & Main sits in the warehouse district's front row — the streetcar picks you up around the corner and drops you at campus, the Mercado, or back home from wherever the night went.

  • Sun Link streetcar stop 2 MIN WALK
  • Congress Street venues 4 MIN WALK
  • Ronstadt Transit Center 5 MIN WALK
  • Mercado San Agustín 12 MIN RIDE
