The homes

Renewed, not replaced.

Good bones from 1970, everything else from this year. Each home was taken back to the brick and rebuilt with the materials the original builders would have chosen if they'd had them — quiet, durable, warm to the touch.

  • Sage-lacquered cabinetry

    Custom-built, soft-close, with unlacquered brass pulls that age like the trees.

  • Alabaster quartz counters

    Deep prep runs and a breakfast ledge in every kitchen.

  • Smart where it counts

    Keyless entry, learning thermostats, and in-unit laundry — the quiet kind.

  • A patio of your own

    Every home opens to a walled garden patio — citrus tree optional, encouraged.

The grounds

Forty years of shade, and everything under it.

  • Saltwater pool

    Under the palms, warm from March to November, quiet by design.

  • Garden plots

    Raised beds along the east wall — tomatoes in February, like Tucson intended.

  • Citrus courtyard

    Orange and grapefruit trees, ramada tables, and a communal harvest every winter.

  • Bike shed & stand

    Covered parking for sixty bikes, a repair stand, and a pump that always works.

  • Dog park

    Shaded, gated, and gossiped about at the water station most mornings.

  • The reading room

    A small library and long table in the old leasing office — swap shelf included.

  • Hammock grove

    Six hammocks, four mesquites, one rule: no meetings.

  • Ramada kitchen

    Two grills, one long table, and standing dibs for birthday dinners.

Midtown

Every errand is a bike ride.

The Paloverde sits in the leafy middle of Midtown Tucson — the neighborhood where the city keeps its parks, its breakfast spots, and its shortest commutes.

  • Reid Park & the zoo 5 min by bike
  • Broadway Village shops 4 min
  • University of Arizona 10 min
  • Banner University Medical 8 min
  • Downtown & the streetcar 12 min
  • Tucson International Airport 20 min
